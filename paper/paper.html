A Computational Exploration of Finite Projection Obstructions and Local Worldline Counterexamples Motivated by Four-Dimensional Kakeya

Runyuan Wang

13 August 2026

Abstract

This paper reports a bounded computational exploration of a finite projection-copy model and a local worldline incidence model motivated by four-dimensional Kakeya. It does not prove or disprove the four-dimensional Kakeya conjecture, and it does not claim a new Kakeya bound. The paper also does not independently rerun the source package: it reports frozen source artifacts after manifest checking and static source-to-output audit. Within a restricted individual-projection copy-and-encode language, a finite search records 1,725 four-atom schemas and 313,762 encodings, and a one-more-copy search records 110,160 eight-atom graphs and 8,931,424 encodings, with no candidate below the classical 7/4 cost in the specified budgets. Separately, an exact rational six-worldline certificate gives an induced strict-time C12 for every δ<1/25 while avoiding the three named local degeneracies; this is a counterexample only to the implication that such a C12 forces a bush, a common affine velocity plane, or a spacetime hyperplane. An exact audit of one selected dual-core near miss gives rank 18 and nullity 2, with both residual modes identified as sibling fibre slides. Numerical entropy regression and bounded beam screens are reported in separate evidence classes, not as exact certificates or exhaustive searches. The outcome is a set of model-specific constraints and two unresolved interfaces, not a continuum conclusion.

1. Scope, evidence classes, and non-claims

The Euclidean Kakeya problem asks how small a set can be while containing a unit line segment in every direction. At the literature-verification date, the introduction of a 2026 v1 preprint states that the Euclidean conjecture remains open in dimensions at least four [1]; that very recent status must be rechecked before any submission. For dimension four, Katz and Zahl obtained a peer-reviewed Hausdorff-dimension lower bound of 3.059 using planebrushes [2]. We do not describe that number as current-best. Katz and Tao's arithmetic-projection work supplies the historical sum–difference and Kakeya lineage for the four-form model [3] [4]. Shared vocabulary does not establish equivalence between their hypotheses and the repository model.

“KT seed” is repository-author terminology for one four-atom constraint pattern, plausibly alluding to the Katz–Tao arithmetic-projection/sum–difference lineage. No literature equivalence is asserted. Likewise, “collision”, “worldline”, “fibre slide”, and “dual core” are internal definitions. A tube intersection is not automatically a physical collision, and graph similarity does not transfer Kakeya estimates.

Scope boundary. Nothing below proves or disproves four-dimensional Kakeya, improves an arithmetic Kakeya constant, establishes priority, or exhausts entropy/copy-lemma methods. Exactness always refers to arithmetic or rank in the stated frozen finite object.

2. Finite projection-copy model

Let an atom be Ui=(Xi,Yi), with four allowed input forms

X,  Y,  C=X+Y,  D=X+2Y,

and target form Z=X−Y. A coloured collision row R(Ui)=R(Uj) identifies one allowed input projection across two atoms. The source code passes to the linear quotient of all collision rows. A cut or encoding h is a tuple of individual allowed projection values. An atom is an anchor when h determines its Z value; the full anchor atom may then pay for remaining conditional ambiguity. A candidate succeeds only if h, together with all full anchor atoms, spans the quotient.

This is a deliberately narrow linear-rank syntax. Its cost counts collision and individual encoding components. It does not model arbitrary joint-entropy discounts, non-Shannon inequalities, long cells that close only after a final twist, or Euclidean multiscale information.

3. E1 Restricted finite projection-copy no-go

  1. Four atoms. All 1,725 two-layer generation schemas of accounted cost at most 7 were tested through 313,762 encoding tests. There are 16 distinct fixed-label cost-7 seed constraint sets and no candidate of cost at most 6.
  2. One more aligned copy layer. For each of the 16 seeds, two copies were coupled over individual allowed projection values. There are 6,885 graphs per seed, hence 110,160 graphs, and 8,931,424 encoding tests under total cost at most 13. No candidate is recorded. The strongest recorded near miss has quotient dimension 8, reconstruction rank 6, anchors 0 and 5, and accounted cost 13.

The 13/8 budget is not presented as a candidate bound. The frozen internal audit classifies it as below the source package's recorded lower-bound window and therefore uses it only as a syntax diagnostic. That time-sensitive lower-bound window was not independently established by the retained external literature set, so this draft makes no external record-status assertion from it.

Rank decisions used the prime 1,000,000,007; the implementation supplies a Hadamard upper bound of 108 for relevant nonzero minors. A separate Fraction-based implementation checks all 1,725 four-atom schemas, all 6,885 extra-copy graphs for the single original seed, and one reported global near miss. It does not independently enumerate the complete 16-seed eight-atom search or every Möbius-style diagnostic.

Sources: finite certificate, main search, and rational verifier.

Scope boundary. The result rules out only the stated two-layer individual-projection schemas and one aligned extra-copy layer in the specified budgets. It does not rule out joint tuple discounts, stronger copy lemmas, long non-rectangular or Möbius structures, non-Shannon inequalities, Euclidean input, or any Kakeya conjecture.

4. E1 Exact rational strict-time C12 local counterexample

For i=0,…,5, define a spacetime segment Li(t)=(t,ci+tvi), 0≤t≤3. Event Qi is the intersection of Li and Li+1, modulo six.

Event times by cycle index are (0,1,2,3,201/100,99/100), giving Q0<Q5<Q1<Q2<Q4<Q3. Exact rational minimization and determinant checks record:

min d²(nonadjacent segments)=2017/55600;
min d²(event, nonincident segment)=529/12200;
min |det(vi,vj,vk)|=7/3000;
min affine quadruple determinant=43/33000.

For every δ<1/25, the certificate therefore gives an induced alternating C12, with no nonconsecutive tube intersection, no three-tube bush, no common affine velocity two-plane, and no common spacetime three-hyperplane.

The named implication refuted is only: every induced strict-time six-worldline C12 forces at least one of a three-tube bush, a common affine velocity two-plane, or a common spacetime three-hyperplane.

This is not a counterexample to Kakeya, C12 abundance, or an extremal graph theorem. Sources: JSON certificate and checker source.

Scope boundary. This draft statically inspected the witness and checker but did not execute them independently. “Exact rational” describes the frozen arithmetic, not independent reproduction.

5. E2 Exact anatomy of one selected dual-core residual

The wider dual-core family was selected and refined using bounded beams. For the strongest recorded ten-atom near miss, the reconstruction system has ten collision rows, seven encoding rows, and two coordinate rows for each of five anchors: 27 rows over 20 coordinates. The encoding values are X0, C4, D5, Y0, Y1, Y2, X8; anchors are 0,1,2,8,9.

rank(A)=18,   dim ker(A)=20−18=2.

A primitive basis is supported on atoms 6 and 7:

e6: (ΔX6,ΔY6)=(1,−1),   e7: (ΔX7,ΔY7)=(1,−1).

Each motion preserves C=X+Y at one sibling C-coloured leaf. Thus the two leaves slide independently while every recorded collision, all seven encoding values, and all five anchor atoms remain fixed.

Sources: exact residual audit and combined certificate.

Scope boundary. Rank 18, nullity 2, and the fibre slides describe this selected near miss. They are not a no-go theorem for all dual-core interfaces.

6. N Entropy-cycle regression, not an exact LP certificate

The source ledger rewrites classical four-form bookkeeping with conditional copies and a three-value cut, yielding the hand-derived inequality

4H(X−Y) ≤ 2H(X)+2H(Y)+2H(X+Y)+H(X+2Y).

This is a collision-cycle interpretation of existing arithmetic-projection bookkeeping, not a claim of a new proof or theorem.

A PSITIP 1.1.7 regression uses OR-Tools 9.15.6755 and floating GLOP. Pairwise functional dependence alone does not prove the weighted inequality. Adding two conditional-copy relations and supplied cut dependencies does. Controls rejecting H(X−Y)≤H(X) and collapse of the assumption cone also pass.

weighted_cycle_cutting.py checks copied supports and cut identities but evaluates entropy in floating point. psitip_cycle_audit.py supplies those identities as functional dependencies and audits the implication afterward. The PSITIP/GLOP output contains no exact rational dual certificate.

8. F Framing and U two unresolved interfaces

Worldlines, multisloped projection fibres, and collision-graph circuit rank provide a common language for asking whether repeated local identifications create global rigidity. That is framing only. A tube incidence need not be a physical collision; exclusion can suppress same-time overlap; and circuit rank alone does not carry entropy, probability, or dimension estimates.

The frozen route matrix leaves:

  1. Worldline-specific soft-mode-to-grain interface. A theorem would need to show that low-frequency or near-kernel sections either generate enough fresh independent rank or synchronize into controlled planar/sticky grains.
  2. Collision compilation/cost plus global-tail interface. A theorem would need to compile a bad tube family into R≈ND chargeable genuine/conditional collision gates, or force the structured branch, and then provide a global large-deviation estimate at the needed scale.

Completing both without additional hypotheses would constitute the central body of a proof, not a routine follow-up. They are project formulations, not literature consensus or feasibility claims.

10. Limitations and conclusion

  • The no-go covers an explicitly restricted individual-projection linear-rank syntax.
  • The C12 witness refutes only the named local forcing implication.
  • The dual-core nullspace is exact only for one selected near miss.
  • GLOP is floating point and no exact rational LP dual was exported.
  • Every beam null is non-exhaustive.
  • Manifest/hash and static audit are not independent reproduction; the source package was not rerun.
  • No bridge theorem transfers the finite/local statements to Euclidean four-dimensional Kakeya.
  • The bounded literature packet supports no priority, novelty, exhaustive-survey, or current-record claim.

This exploration records three exact but bounded outcomes: a no-go inside a restricted finite projection-copy syntax, an exact rational counterexample to one named strict-time C12 forcing implication, and rank-18/nullity-2 anatomy for one selected dual-core residual. It also records a floating entropy regression and bounded route-pruning screens. These constrain the examined models; they do not prove or disprove four-dimensional Kakeya, do not establish a new bound, and do not exhaust untested proof languages. The source package was not independently rerun.
